The experiment uses the original 20-control mirrored fillet in the established planar Re 50 Taylor–Hood Brinkman model. The original design is the vector with all entries 0.5. Controls remain in [0,1] and act over \(y=5.0\)–\(6.5\) mm. The design map fixes the inlet, outlets, main bore, guide and join geometry. The minimum passage width is 1.00 mm by construction. The 26-control area convention was approved for the diagnostic pilot. The metering reach has measured finite secants and lacks a verified adjoint for those fitted-wall controls. Neither later family is used as the optimization formulation in this study. ## Experimental questions and gates

- **Derivatives and mathematical prediction.** Rerun the existing centered-difference and Taylor tests for dissipation, side share, pressure and area using the archived step sequence and acceptance rules. Evaluate independent PDE states along an adjoint-selected descent step at several sizes. Compare measured differences to baseline gradients times the displacement. A passing local prediction does not establish finite-step feasibility.
- **Constrained updates.** Apply the existing SLSQP method to the approved objective with the original inequalities. A separately labeled feasibility-restoration problem may supply an initial point or a diagnostic result. Its progress cannot be used as engineering feasibility or KKT evidence for the original problem. Preserve successful and failed trial outcomes, major iterates, derivatives, actual and predicted changes, pressure, flow, area and constraint residuals. Only compute engineering stationarity at an observed feasible point.
- **Independent governing-equation verification.** Hold the archived original contour and the predeclared selected comparison contour fixed. Evaluate both with the existing fitted-wall FEM and common geometry and boundary conditions on three meshes. If the hard-constrained comparison has an objective at or above the baseline, also evaluate the prespecified adjoint-descent contour \(a_0-0.04\nabla J(a_0)/\|\nabla J(a_0)\|_\infty\). Report this third contour as a separate prediction diagnostic. Report side and main flows, static pressure, viscous dissipation, physical area and width, all relevant margins, and uncertainty in the paired change. Apply the numerical verification record's refinement checks. The fitted solve excludes Brinkman work, so baseline-relative fitted viscous dissipation and the optimization objective are distinct measurements.
- **Independent cross-code evidence.** The archived OpenFOAM work checks laminar discretization, mesh effects and the outlet formulation differences. It did not evaluate the new comparison geometry. Reserve a new OpenFOAM run for a decision-critical result after fitted verification, such as a candidate in the flow band with an unresolved pressure classification. Keep the differing outlet formulations visible. [OpenFOAM record](next_openfoam/OPENFOAM_VALIDATION.md)

## Role and resource boundaries

Mathematical optimization owns its isolated driver, predictions and iteration history. Numerical verification owns a separate fitted solve and numerical audit. Independent compliance reviews both protocols before computation. The lead coordinates the bounded Sol submissions and will assign independent HPC and CFD audits as worker slots become available. Historical sources and results remain read-only. Use one BLAS/OpenMP thread per process, parallelize only independent work, and record exact source hashes, runtime, CPU-hours, memory, and failures.
